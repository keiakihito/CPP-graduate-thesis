  Point 2 — Neuroscience
                        
  This is my best guess at what the advisor means, and it actually connects well:
                                                                                                       
  There's established neuroscience research showing that music listening activates the brain's reward  
  system (dopamine release), and that the reward is strongest for music that is "just right" — familiar
   enough to be pleasant but novel enough to be interesting. This is sometimes called the "sweet spot" 
  between predictability and surprise — related to concepts like optimal complexity or the Goldilocks
  principle in perception research.

  The connection to your work:

  ▎ "Neuroscience tells us why serendipity matters: the brain rewards musical discovery in the         
  ▎ familiar-but-novel sweet spot. A recommendation system that only returns songs you already know 
  ▎ saturates that response. One that returns totally random music misses it. The system needs to find 
  ▎ that sweet spot — which requires capturing genuine musical similarity. That's exactly what 
  ▎ embedding quality controls."

  This frames your work as not just an engineering problem but as something grounded in why human music
   listening works the way it does. It elevates the motivation from "iPalpiti needs a feature" to
  "we're trying to engineer the conditions for musical discovery."
